Hey, welcome in. I'm Vishnu, and this is the place where I'll be writing down everything I learn (and break) on my way through cybersecurity.

I've wanted to start a blog like this for a while now. Every time I solved a machine or dug into a vulnerability, I'd end up with a messy pile of notes, half-saved commands and screenshots scattered across folders. A few weeks later I'd forget how I actually got that foothold, or why a particular exploit worked. So this blog is partly for you, and honestly, partly for future me.

## Why I'm doing this

There's a saying that you don't really understand something until you can explain it to someone else. I've found that to be painfully true in security. It's easy to copy a payload, get a shell and move on. It's a lot harder to sit down and explain *why* that payload worked, what the developer got wrong, and how it could have been prevented.

Writing forces me to slow down and actually understand things. If a post here helps someone else get unstuck on a box or finally make sense of a CVE, that's a bonus I'll gladly take.

## What you'll find here

I'm going to keep things practical. Here's roughly what to expect:

- **Lab walkthroughs** – Solutions to PortSwigger Web Security Academy labs. I'm working through them topic by topic, starting with access control and authentication, and I explain the vulnerability behind each lab, not just the clicks that solve it.
- **Machine writeups** – Step-by-step walkthroughs of TryHackMe rooms and other machines I've solved, from enumeration to root. I'll try to show my actual thought process, including the rabbit holes and dead ends, not just the clean path that worked in the end.
- **CVE analysis** – Breakdowns of real-world vulnerabilities: what the bug is, where it lives in the code, how it can be exploited, and how it was patched.
- **Tools** – Notes on tools I use regularly, useful flags I keep forgetting, and small scripts I write to save myself time.
- **Notes** – Concepts, cheat sheets and things I'm studying. Some of these will be rough, but I'd rather share them than let them rot in a private folder.

## A quick note on ethics

Everything I write about here is for learning and for authorized testing only. The machines I cover are from legal practice platforms or lab environments, and any vulnerability research is done responsibly. Please don't take anything from this blog and point it at systems you don't own or don't have permission to test. That's not hacking, that's just a crime.

## I'm still learning

I'm not writing this as an expert who has it all figured out. I'm learning as I go, and I'll definitely get things wrong sometimes. If you spot a mistake, know a better way to do something, or just want to talk security, feel free to reach out. I'd genuinely appreciate it.

## What's next

A bunch of PortSwigger lab writeups and my first TryHackMe notes are already up, so feel free to dig in. There's a lot more on the way. Grab a coffee, open a terminal, and let's learn something together.

Thanks for stopping by.

— Vishnu
